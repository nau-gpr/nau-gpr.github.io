## Biography
My purpose in life revolves around a central theme of helping others rekindle their resonance with the natural world while remembering what it means to be fully human in this historical moment. I do this through teaching, facilitating, guiding, and coaching. My path began as a young man growing up on the edges of Philadelphia while wildly curious about the natural world. A blind finger drop on a map landed me in Flagstaff, AZ, where I completed my studies in outdoor education and leadership through the Parks and Recreation Management (PRM) program at Northern Arizona University (NAU). After an eight-year hiatus of wandering and wondering both physically and psychologically, I returned to Flagstaff from my Peace Corps time in Guatemala to earn a Masters in Sustainable Communities. I was privileged to get an opportunity as a professor in the same undergraduate program I completed years earlier. In that position, I taught numerous courses at various levels while also developing nature-based transformative curriculum for college students and community members. Since then, I have continued to teach in the PRM program while also guiding nature-based rites of passage for the Animas Valley Institute. As my perspectives evolved, I chose to once again return to school to earn a doctorate in Ecopsychology and Environmental Humanities. This most recent step was challenging while working full time and welcoming a new baby into the world. Yet, my passions burn even brighter allowing me to show up as best I can as a father, partner, teacher, guide, and mentor. My path has helped me develop skills such as critical thinking, self reflection, expanded epistemologies, writing, ecopsychological analysis, and communications that help foster the right kinds of questions to help myself and others step closer towards becoming visionary artisans of social/cultural transformation. It has been a wonderous pleasure to follow this thread of human/nature relationship and I hope to continue evolving how I show up in that way.

## Interests
### Teaching
- For the last sixteen years I have been teaching students and public members in a variety of capacities. My passions are primarily aligned with teaching others about ecological relationality and climate justice for the purposes cultivating visionary agents of cultural transformation on behalf of all life. My teaching approaches favor experiential learning, Socratic discussion, and self-reflexive designed lessons aimed at growing student awareness and relationality. All of my academic degrees are interdisciplinary helping me to teach across a wide spectrum of topics. I am currently affiliated with the Geography, Planning, and Recreation department and the Sustainable Communities program at NAU. Both areas share a concern with land/nature relationships through a multiplicity of academic lenses.

### Research
- My last research project explored the integration of alternative epistemologies into academic learning environment through theories and practices steeped in ecopsychology. This was a project that completed my doctorate in ecopsychology and environmental humanities.
