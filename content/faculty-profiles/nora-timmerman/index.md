## Interests
### Teaching
- For 20 years I have been teaching in formal and informal adult educational environments. My passion is to teach about social and ecological justice within local communities and places for societal and cultural transformation. My pedagogical approach uses active, experiential, and self-reflexive learning designed to encourage growth within students, the community, and myself as instructor. My interdisciplinary background prepares me to teach in a range of fields. Currently, I teach within and coordinate the Sustainable Communities Program, a hub for a MA degree, minor in Community Engagement, and program for collaborative research with local community organizations committed to just sustainability. The SUS program focuses on the sociocultural roots of our social & ecological injustices/crises and how we can work together to address these through community action and organizing.
### Research
- My research interests are in environmental education, higher education, activism, and social change. I utilize broad conceptions of education that examine its role as a socio-cultural institution. In particular, I use critical ecofeminist theory, Piven's theory of interdependent and disruptive power in social movements, and critical place-based theories to ground my work. I tend to approach research qualitatively and gravitate toward literary non-fiction writing and research representation. Currently, I am writing up the results of a research project funded by the Society for Research into Higher Education examining historical cases of disruptive, collaborative faculty activism for social movement issues. I intend to use the results of this research to continue and inquiry into the possibilities for and barriers to collective action for climate justice among present-day academics.
